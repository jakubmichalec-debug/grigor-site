Motion & Interaction Spec:
layrmedia.com

Deep spec — preloader & audio gate · WebGL model · video hero · camera parallax
Plus motion inventory for all five pages

layrmedia.com is a Next.js site for a Toronto photography and video studio, built by Lumina. Its motion design rests on one idea: the site behaves like a piece of film equipment. You are gated at the door and asked whether you want sound, an object loads before the page does, and almost every subsequent transition is driven by scroll position rather than by a clock. Nothing plays at you; you crank it.

This document specifies that behaviour precisely enough to rebuild. It covers the global motion system first, then the four subsystems in depth, then a page-by-page inventory, and closes with performance, accessibility and QA requirements.

How to read this document

Every row is tagged for provenance. OBS means the behaviour is directly observable on the live site. INF means the behaviour is inferred from the markup, asset names and loading order, and should be confirmed against the original before it becomes acceptance criteria.

Durations and easings are prescriptive targets, not measurements pulled from the source. They are tuned to reproduce the observed feel. Treat them as the spec to build to; a developer can move any of them within ±20% without breaking the system.

2 — Preloader & audio gate

OBS. The gate is global: it appears in the markup of every page, not just the homepage. Its copy runs [ Loading Model... ] → Creative Production Studio → Enter with audio / [ Enter without sound ]. The header then carries a persistent [ Sound: Off ] toggle.

Two jobs in one screen. It hides the cost of a 3D model and a video, and it collects the one click a browser needs before it will let audio play. Build it as a single fixed layer above everything, mounted by the root layout, driven by a real asset-loading promise — never a fake timer.

2.2 Audio state contract

The choice at the gate is the only thing that can unmute audio, and it must survive navigation. Hold it in a context provider at the root, mirrored to sessionStorage.

  • Enter with audio → state on. Call video.play() and unmute inside the click handler itself, in the same task — a deferred call loses the user-gesture grant and Safari will reject it.
  • Enter without sound → state off. Video still plays, muted.
  • Header toggle reads [ Sound: Off ] / [ Sound: On ]. Crossfade the label 200ms; ramp gain over 400ms rather than cutting, so a mid-scroll toggle does not pop.
  • Repeat visits within a session skip the gate entirely and run only the curtain lift (step 05, 1000ms). Mark the session flag on first successful entry.
  • Auto-mute on visibilitychange to hidden; restore on return only if the state was on.

Required fallbacks

Hard timeout: dismiss the preloader after 6s regardless of load state, and let the model pop in when ready. If WebGL is unavailable or the GLTF fails, skip the "Loading Model" copy and show the gate immediately. Under prefers-reduced-motion, keep the gate but replace the curtain with a 200ms opacity fade.

3 — WebGL model

OBS. A 3D model is loaded before entry on every route — the loader names it explicitly. On the homepage the section that follows the hero carries the interaction hints click and Scroll to view, alongside a still thumbnail.

INF — confirm with the original. The subject is a camera body, consistent with the separate 2D camera layers used further down the page (§5). Where the canvas renders — a single persistent canvas in the root layout versus a per-section canvas — is the one architectural question this spec cannot answer from the outside. Build it persistent; it is the only way the model can survive a route change, and the gate loading it globally strongly suggests that is what happens.

Element Spec

4 — Video hero

OBS. Full-bleed background video at /home/hero/1.mp4, headline LAYR.   MEDIA set with a wide internal gap, a duplicated Scroll cue, and twelve stills at /home/hero/1–12.jpg that also appear on the Services page. The duplicated cue and duplicated headline text are the signature of a per-character or per-line split, where the second copy is the measuring or masked layer.

4.1 Video element

  • muted playsinline loop preload="auto" with a poster from the stills set. object-fit: cover, will-change: transform.
  • Deliver two encodes: 1920×1080 H.264 at ≤4Mbps for desktop, 1280×720 at ≤1.8Mbps for mobile, plus a WebM/VP9 alternate. Loop point must be seamless — first and last frame identical.
  • Playback starts during the preloader, paused at frame 0. On gate dismissal, play() and unmute if audio is on.
  • Pause when the hero leaves the viewport (IntersectionObserver, threshold 0) and when the tab is hidden. Resume on re-entry, do not restart.
  • On Save-Data or a 2G connection, drop to the poster still. On prefers-reduced-motion, keep the video but remove the scroll-linked scale.

4.3 Scroll-out

Scroll-linked, no easing. Progress measured from hero top to hero bottom, mapped over one viewport height.

  • Video scale 1.00 → 1.12 and y 0 → 12% for depth; headline y 0 → −18% at a faster rate, so the type outruns the footage.
  • Headline opacity 1 → 0 across progress 0.35 → 0.7. Scroll cue fades out over 0 → 0.15.
  • Hero clip-path inset closes from 0 to 6% on all sides as the next section arrives, so the video reads as a card sliding under the fold rather than a hard cut.

5 — Camera parallax layers

OBS. Two transparent PNGs, /home/camera/p1.png and p2.png, labelled "Camera layer 1" and "Camera layer 2", sit immediately before the We're LAYR. block. A cut-out object split across two files means one thing: the copy is sandwiched between them, so text passes through the object as you scroll.

5.2 Implementation requirements

  • Pin the section for 160vh of scroll. Every layer is position:absolute inside it; only transform and opacity animate.
  • Percentage translations, not pixels — the depth ratio must hold from 1280 to 2560 wide.
  • Pointer tilt is damped at lerp 0.08 and applied on top of scroll transforms via a wrapper element, so the two systems never fight over one transform string.
  • Both PNGs need priority loading — a late foreground layer is a visible pop. Ship them at 2× the largest rendered size, no more.
  • Under 900px, collapse to a single layer with a 40% reduced range; drop the tilt entirely on touch.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: no pin, no parallax. Static composition, copy fades in.

7 — Performance & accessibility

This much motion only works if the frame budget holds. A WebGL canvas, an autoplaying video, several always-running marquees and half a dozen pinned scroll sections all compete for the same main thread, and the site's own entry sequence is what buys the headroom.

7.1 Budget

  • Animate transform and opacity only. No animated width, height, top, left, filter or box-shadow anywhere in this spec.
  • One requestAnimationFrame loop for the whole page, shared by the scroll engine, marquees and the WebGL canvas. Never one per component.
  • Pause off-screen work: marquee animation-play-state, video playback, and the canvas frame loop all gate on IntersectionObserver.
  • will-change: transform only on currently-animating layers; remove it after. Roughly 40 hero and gallery images plus a canvas will exhaust texture memory if every one is promoted.
  • Targets: 60fps sustained on an M-series laptop, ≥45fps on a mid-range Android. LCP under 2.5s measured from gate dismissal, not page load.
  • Lazy-load every Vimeo thumbnail below the fold. The homepage repeats ten of them eight times over — the repeats must reuse one cached request each, not re-fetch.

7.2 Accessibility requirements

  • prefers-reduced-motion: reduce must be honoured globally: no smooth-scroll hijack, no pins, no parallax, no marquees. Replace every reveal with a 200ms opacity fade. Content and reading order stay identical.
  • Nothing may be gated behind hover or scroll alone. Every scroll-revealed section is present and readable with JavaScript disabled or scroll broken.
  • The audio gate needs a focus trap, a visible focus ring, Esc as "enter without sound", and a real heading — not just styled text.
  • The infinite canvas needs a keyboard path: arrow keys pan, Tab moves item to item and brings the focused item into view. Provide a plain list fallback.
  • Never rely on the marquee to deliver information. "PHOTO. VIDEO. CONTENT." is decorative — the static copy beside it carries the meaning.

7.3 QA checklist

  • Refresh mid-scroll: pins re-measure, no dead space, no stacked sections.
  • Resize across the 900px breakpoint while a pinned section is active.
  • Back/forward navigation: canvas persists, audio state persists, gate does not reappear.
  • Enter with audio in Safari — confirm the unmute survives, since it is the strictest gate.
  • Throttle to 3G: gate must still dismiss at the 6s timeout with the model absent.
  • Reduced motion on: scroll to the bottom of every page and confirm nothing is missing or clipped.
  • Tab through every page start to finish; confirm focus never enters a hidden overlay.
